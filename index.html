Conflicts & open questions

  • Parodi Cigar Factory location — Tim: Baltimore. Find a Grave bio: Scranton. Company histories place Parodi’s Scranton plant from 1930; no Baltimore plant found. If Angelina rolled cigars for Parodi it was most likely in Scranton after 1930. Baltimore is where she died. [7] [11] [12] [13]
  • Giovanni’s birthplace — Tim: Perugia region. Records found: “Italy” only (SSA Numident abstract). Not a contradiction, but unverified. Context: son-in-law Giuseppe Incociati was born in Perugia; the Biscontini surname clusters in the Province of Perugia. [2] [8] [9]
  • “Gussie” = Louis J. Guzzi? — Tim: Irma married “Gussie”. Records: Irma’s husband was Louis J. Guzzi (1894–1978). “Gussie” is likely a nickname from the surname (Assumed, likely). [6] [22]
  • Eva’s surname — Wittick (Tim) / Winnick (first heard) vs Widdick (SSA, Find a Grave, Baltimore Sun). [37] [4]
  • Tim’s “Luis → Olga, Eva” recollection — Olga and Eva are records-proven daughters of Giovanni and Angelina (Alice’s sisters). Eva Widdick’s 2005 obituary names daughters Angela Rose, Frances Widdick, Linda Smith and Maria Brauer — matching Tim’s four daughters. Daughters placed under Eva Widdick; sisters not moved; “Luis” kept as a flagged note (possibly Louis J. Guzzi). [37] [22]
  • Marie vs Maria — Tim: Marie. Obituary: Maria Brauer. [37]
  • Olga’s surname — Oberholzer (heard) vs Oberholtzer (records). [34] [3]
  • Rigo/Rico vs Merico “Rego” — Same person; record given name Merico, nickname Rego. [21]
  • Dora’s birth year — 1901 (Find a Grave family lists) vs 1909 (SSA death index entry for Dora Barbetti, Old Forge). [14] [15]
  • Irma’s death place and birth date — Find a Grave: died Lackawanna Co., Pa. Death index: Maryland. Birth 14 Aug 1913 vs 14 Apr 1912 in a second index entry. [6] [23]
  • Mary Lero vs Mary Ann Lerro — Tim spells Lero; records spell Lerro. Birth 11 Jun (Find a Grave) vs 13 Jun 1915 (SSA). [41] [28]
  • Louise Aulisio — Tim says Thomas’s sister was Mary Lero, not Louise Alessio/Aulisio. Records (father’s Find a Grave children list; mother’s 1957 obituary “Mrs. Dominick Aulisio, Newark”) indicate Louise was also a sister. Shown as possible additional sibling, unconfirmed by family. [27] [38] [42]
  • Patrick Joseph “Papp” — Tim: Patrick Joseph Papp, married Mary Febbo. Assumed to be Patrick Joseph Mazzaccaro (1920–1968), whose wife was Mary Febbo. [43] [44]
  • Pat Jr. — Family: possible child, unconfirmed. Records: Mary Febbo’s obituary names Patrick Mazzaccaro Jr. as her late son. [44] [45]
  • Surname spelling Mazzacaro / Mazzaccaro / Mazzacarro / Mazzucaro — Family usage Mazzacaro; records overwhelmingly Mazzaccaro (double c); Alice’s obituary has “Mazzacarro”; Alice’s memorial spells her maiden name “Anderoni”. [26] [25] [24]
  • “AMP” vs A&P — Norma said Doc was a supervisor at “AMP”; his obituary says A&P. [30]
  • Dominic vs Dominick; Tonyann vs Toni Ann; Lindsey vs Lyndsey — Interview spellings differ from Doc’s 2024 obituary. [30]
  • Deli story / which Anthony — Who died during the deli period and whether the “Anthony” is son Anthony T. (d. 1995) is unresolved. Details in the interview-context document. [1]
  • John Mazzaccaro’s birthplace — Find a Grave gives “Castel, Provincia di Aosta” — implausible for a southern-Italian surname; treated as Italy only. [27]
  • Sibling count — Earlier tree draft showed 9 / Tim expected 7. With Gussie removed (he was Irma’s husband), the interview list has 8 children of Giovanni and Angelina, matching the 8 in records. [6] [1]

Data standards used

  • Names: Given names then SURNAME; women are recorded under their birth (maiden) surname with married surnames listed separately (GEDCOM NAME “Given /Surname/”, with married names as an additional NAME of TYPE married). Family-used names (“Mary Lero”, “Eva Wittick”) are shown as the card label, with record spellings and variants listed on the card.
  • Dates: DD Mon YYYY (e.g. 30 May 1877); qualifiers ABT (about), BEF (before), AFT (after), BET … AND … as in GEDCOM 5.5.1. Partial dates kept as given (1880, Aug 1978).
  • Places: Smallest to largest, comma-separated: City, County, State, Country (e.g. Old Forge, Lackawanna, Pennsylvania, USA). Unknown levels are omitted.
  • IDs: Every person has a stable unique ID (I = Andreoni side, M = Mazzaccaro, A = Azzolini, N = Norma’s generation, C = Norma’s children, G = grandchildren). The same IDs are used as @I…@ record IDs in family-tree.ged.
  • Citations: Each assertion is tied to its own source (Evidence Explained principle: cite each individual assertion to its own source). Sources are numbered in the Sources list with title, repository/website and URL.
  • Evidence tags: Interview = told by Norma via Tim; Record = found in a public record or record abstract (with link); Assumed = a researcher inference; Conflict = sources disagree — both sides shown, conflict stated, and the preferred reading explained (BCG Genealogy Standards: conflicting evidence is shown, not hidden).
  • Confidence: confirmed (direct, consistent records), likely (secondary or user-contributed sources that agree), unconfirmed (single, indirect, or family-only). Maps to GEDCOM QUAY 3 / 2 / 1.
  • Living people: Possibly-living people show names and relationships only (no dates or places), and are exported to GEDCOM as “Living” with no details (FamilySearch privacy guidance). No addresses, contact details, or identification numbers.
  • Chart: Descendant (drop-line) charts: one chart per ancestral line, oldest generation at top, children in birth order left-to-right where known, spouses side by side joined by ⚭, double-surname lines linked across charts.
